The Plastic Crisis

Plastic pollution has become one of the most pressing environmental issues, as rapidly increasing production of disposable plastic products overwhelms our world's ability to deal with them.

It impacts our health, wildlife, and the climate. Plastic waste is now found in the most remote areas of the planet. It kills millions of marine animals every year from entanglement and starvation.

Marine Life at Risk

  • Over 800 species are known to be affected by marine litter.
  • Plastic ingestion causes malnutrition and internal injuries in seabirds and turtles.
  • Ghost fishing gear and nets entangle and drown countless marine mammals.

Microplastics

  • Tiny plastic fragments are now found in our food, water, and air.
  • They absorb and concentrate toxic chemicals from the surrounding seawater.
  • Microplastics can cross biological barriers and accumulate in human organs.

What Causes Plastic Pollution?

  • Single-Use Plastics

    Items meant to be used just once constitute the vast majority of plastic waste.

  • Industrial Waste

    Significant amounts of plastic waste originate from manufacturing and industrial fishing.

  • Poor Waste Management

    Inadequate garbage disposal infrastructure leads to direct leakage into the environment.

The Devastating Effects

The consequences of plastic pollution are far-reaching.

Ocean Acidification

As plastics degrade, they release chemicals that alter the ocean's chemistry, severely affecting marine ecosystems.

Wildlife Fatalities

Turtles, seabirds, and whales mistake plastic for food, causing malnutrition, intestinal blockage, and death.

Human Health

Microplastics enter the food chain, carrying toxic chemicals that ultimately end up on our plates.

Be Part of the Solution

We can all contribute to stopping plastic pollution. Small changes in our daily habits make a massive difference globally.

Reduce, Reuse, Recycle

  • Reduce: Prioritize choosing products with minimal or biodegradable packaging to cut down overall consumption.
  • Reuse: Switch to high-quality reusable bags, water bottles, coffee cups, and food containers.
  • Recycle: When using disposable items, properly wash and sort them according to local guidelines.
  • Impact: These simple habits can help a household eliminate hundreds of pounds of plastic waste annually.

Support Cleanups

  • Active Participation: Join beach, park, or river initiatives to physically remove waste from natural ecosystems.
  • Protect Wildlife: Grassroots cleanups directly protect local wildlife from immediate entanglement dangers.
  • Gather Data: Help researchers gather critical data on the pervasive types of plastic pollution in your area.
  • Lead by Example: If no local group exists, take the initiative to organize your own community cleanup event.

Advocate for Policies

  • Support Legislation: Back laws that ban or heavily tax harmful single-use plastics.
  • Corporate Accountability: Favor policies holding corporations responsible for packaging waste through EPR programs.
  • Use Your Voice: Actively sign petitions and write to local representatives demanding stronger environmental protections.
  • Vote: Support eco-conscious leaders who prioritize the systemic fight against plastic pollution.
